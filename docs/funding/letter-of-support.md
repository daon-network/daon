# DAON — Letter of Support (for the NGI Zero Commons application)

Purpose: put a named European stakeholder on the application to strengthen the European-dimension gate. A signed letter of support is an email ask, not a hiring problem. Make it near-zero-effort for the org by handing them a draft they can adapt.

---

## Who to ask (ranked by fit — verify current contacts)

1. **European Writers' Council (EWC)** — Brussels; represents authors' associations across Europe; very active on AI & authorship. Best fit for text/authorship provenance.
2. **Open Future Foundation** — Brussels/Warsaw; policy for the digital commons and the open movement. Strong NGI-vision alignment.
3. **COMMUNIA Association** — European advocacy for the digital public domain / commons.
4. **European Digital Rights (EDRi)** — Brussels; digital rights. Less authorship-specific, strong NGI fit.

Also viable: a national authors' society or writers' union (a German one pairs naturally with the EU-hosted service), or a Creative Commons Europe chapter.

> Once any org says yes, **name them in the proposal's European-dimension section** — that converts the gate from a judgment call back toward a checkbox.

## Ready-to-sign draft (org adapts, puts on letterhead)

> Keep only claims the org actually agrees to. The "willing to advise / pilot" line is optional but is the part that most strengthens the European dimension — include it only if they mean it.

[Organisation letterhead] · [Date]

To the NGI Zero Commons Fund, NLnet Foundation

**Re: Letter of support for DAON — Open Provenance and Authorship Attestation for Creative Work**

[Organisation] is [one line: a European organisation representing / advocating for … , based in [country / Brussels]]. We write in support of DAON's application to the NGI Zero Commons Fund.

European authors and creators face an urgent and worsening problem. As automated generation makes authorship disputes routine, creators have no portable, creator-held proof of the origin and revision history of their work — and existing provenance schemes are anchored in devices and vendor-issued certificates rather than in the creator. The EU AI Act's transparency obligations sharpen this need. Infrastructure that is open, vendor-neutral, and owned by no single platform is squarely in the interest of the European creators we represent.

DAON's approach — creator-held keys, an open specification, and a deliberate refusal to build platform-side gatekeeping or "purity" scoring — aligns with our mission and with the interests of European creators. We are glad to see it developed as recognised open-source infrastructure for the commons rather than a proprietary service.

[Optional — keep only if true:] We are willing to advise the project on the needs of European authors and to help pilot the reference implementation with our members and community.

We support this application and hope the Fund will look on it favourably.

Sincerely,
[Name], [Title] — [Organisation]
